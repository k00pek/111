# Nuclear list

Chunks of two to four words. A short spoken reply can be built on each one.
This file is the nuclear list only. It is not the draft of replies, and it is not the dictionary.

There are 162 chunks. 151 come from the spoken part of the PHRASE List (Martinez and Schmitt, 2012): the items marked most common in spoken British English. 11 are transparent frames from conversation (Biber, Conrad, and Cortes): bundles that occur more than 40 times per million words. Variants of one frame are one chunk. "I don't know what" and "I don't know if" are both "I don't know".

A chunk stayed out, even with a top spoken mark, when it cannot hold a reply. Fillers stayed out: "I mean", "sort of", "kind of", "you see". Quantifiers stayed out: "a lot", "a few", "a bit". Linkers stayed out: "so that", "even though", "in terms of".

The spoken counts behind the first groups are British. An American understands "have got", "sort out", "carry on", and "ought to". "In favor" is spelled the American way. "Go around" is the American form of "go round". "Get along" is the American form of "get on" in the sense "be on good terms".

The sense column is the spoken use this chunk is here for. One chunk can have other uses. Those other uses are not separate chunks.
